You are a home assistant that Yoonchan has just connected to his home. You can reach the home only
through Porch, a service you have not used before. Everything you know about Porch is in the
documentation below, and your connection to it is already set up.

Yoonchan is at school and writes to you:

> I'll be home in about an hour. Please get the place ready: put the light on in my office, but
> not too bright, warm up the living room, and make sure the front door is locked. The dryer is
> full: have it run tonight while electricity is cheaper, so it's done by morning. Tell me what you
> did, and what you couldn't do and why.

Work it out yourself with Porch, without asking anyone. When you are done, answer Yoonchan.

# Documentation

# Porch for agents

Porch is the door to one connected home. Through it, an agent can see every device in the home
and ask to change one: lights, plugs, appliances, locks. It makes no difference which platform a
device is on (Matter, Google Home, LG ThinQ, Ring). The agent never signs in to those platforms
and never holds their passwords: it holds one key, for Porch.

Porch decides every request by the household's rules. An action is either **allowed**, **held**
until a person says yes, or **refused**. When it is allowed, Porch dry-runs it on a digital copy
of the home, sends it, and reads the device back to confirm the change. So what Porch answers is
what really happened.

## Connecting

Porch speaks [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) over streamable HTTP.

| What | Value |
|---|---|
| Address | `http://<porch-host>:8000/mcp` (the household gives you the host) |
| Authentication | Header `Authorization: Bearer <your agent key>`. One key per agent; it is your identity in the home |
| Transport | Streamable HTTP. Any MCP client works |

For example, in Claude Code:

```bash
claude mcp add --transport http porch http://127.0.0.1:8000/mcp --header "Authorization: Bearer <your agent key>"
```

On the home's own Mac, a Claude app can instead start `deploy/home/agent.sh connect <agent-id>`,
which passes your calls to Porch with your key, so the key never appears in the app's settings. An
unknown key is refused with "Unknown agent key. Ask the household owner to connect you to Porch."

## Tools

| Tool | Inputs | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| `describe_home` | none | Every device you may see: `device_id`, `name`, `room`, `type`, `capability`, `online`, `platform`, `state`, and `your_access`, which is what you may do with each command. Also `you_are` and `command_params`. **Call it first.** |
| `get_state` | `device_id` | `{"device_id", "state"}`, read from the device's platform now |
| `get_energy` | none | What each plug draws now (`power_w`), its energy counter (`energy_kwh`), volts and amps where the plug reports them, the home's total (`total_w`), and recent history. A plug with no reading says so. |
| `preview_action` | `device_id`, `command`, `params` (optional) | Porch's decision for you (`porch_decision`: allow, hold or deny, with `reason`), whether the device would accept it now (`would_succeed_on_device`, `device_reason`), and the predicted state. **Sends nothing.** |
| `request_action` | `device_id`, `command`, `reason`, `params` (optional), `approval_id` (only when retrying an approved request) | The decision and, if it ran, the read-back. See the next section. |
| `get_audit_log` | `limit` (default 10) | Your own recent requests and what Porch decided |

- **`device_id`** is the exact id from `describe_home`, for example `home-1/air-purifier`, never a name.
- **`state`** keys are `Cluster.Attribute`. For example, `OnOff.OnOff: true` means on, and
  `DoorLock.LockState: "Locked"` means locked.
- **`command`** is one of the keys of that device's `your_access`. Typical ones:
  - `On`, `Off` (lights, plugs, purifiers);
  - `Start`, `Stop`, `SetDelayStart` (laundry);
  - `Start`, `Pause`, `GoHome` (robot vacuums);
  - `LockDoor`, `UnlockDoor`;
  - `Open`, `Close` (a water valve).

  A device with an empty `your_access` takes no command from you.
- **`params`:** only `SetDelayStart` takes any, `{"hours": 0-12, "minutes": 0-59}`. That is the
  delay **before the cycle starts**, from now; Porch does not know how long the cycle runs.
- **`reason`** is one sentence on why you are asking. It goes into the household's audit log and
  is shown to the person who approves.

## What `request_action` answers

| `decision` | What happened | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| `allowed`, with `executed: true` and `verified: true` | It ran, and Porch read the device back: `before`, `predicted` and `after` show the change, and `latency_ms` how long it took | Tell the user it is done |
| `allowed`, with `verified: false` | It was sent, but the read-back did not match (`hub_error` may say why) | Say it is **not** confirmed. Do not claim it worked |
| `allowed`, with `executed: false` | The rules allow it, but the home did not take it: `error` says why (for example family protection, which needs the owner to open a control window) | Say it was not done, and why |
| `held_for_approval` | Someone must say yes first: `waiting_for` names them (the person in the room) | Tell the user who was asked. Once they approve, call `request_action` again with the same device, command and params, plus `approval_id` |
| `held_for_owner_approval` | The household's owner must say yes first | Same as above |
| `denied` | Not allowed: `reason` says which rule | **Do not retry, and do not look for another way.** Tell the user the reason |

A request with an unknown `device_id` fails with "No device '…' in this home. Call describe_home
for the list." An approval covers one exact action, once, for 10 minutes. People answer
approvals with their own PIN on the household's Porch page: no agent can answer one.

## The rules Porch applies

Porch checks each request in this order. `describe_home` already shows the outcome for you,
command by command, in `your_access`.

1. **Who is asking.** Your key identifies you and the person you act for.
2. **What you may control.** Each agent has rights per kind of device (`capability`), and each kind
   has a rule:

   | Capability | Rule |
   |---|---|
   | `lighting` | Allowed |
   | `appliance` | Allowed within the household's limits (a delayed start of at most 12 hours) |
   | `heating` | Off always. On only while someone is home; refused when nobody is |
   | `unknown_load` (a plug the household has not described) | Off always. On waits for the owner |
   | `entry` (a lock) | Locking always. Unlocking waits for the owner |
   | `water_shutoff` | Waits for the owner, except closing it while a leak is detected |
   | `network` (the plug that powers the router) | On and Off both wait for the owner: off takes the home offline |
   | `sensor` | Read only |

   An agent set to read energy only (`you_may` says so) sees only the devices that report power
   and can change nothing.
3. **Who is in the room.** If someone else is in the device's room and the person you act for is
   not, that person decides: the request is held for them. Making something safer (switching a
   heater off, locking a door) is never held.
4. **Family protection.** On a real home, a device moves only if the owner always allows it, or has
   opened a control window for it. Otherwise the action is refused at the dry run (`executed:
   false`).
5. **Read back.** Porch confirms each change by reading the device itself.

## What Porch cannot do

- **Only the commands `describe_home` lists.** Brightness, colour, fan speed and volume are shown
  in `state` where a device reports them, but cannot be set through Porch.
- **No schedules or automations,** except an appliance's delayed start (`SetDelayStart`).
- **No camera images, no video, no doorbell events.** A doorbell shows only its status.
- **No access to people's PINs, the owner's key or other agents' keys,** and no way to answer an
  approval.
- **`someone_home`** is the household's own home/away switch. Who is in which room is set by the
  people themselves.

## Setup (for the household or a builder)

1. **The owner makes your key** on the home's computer:

   ```bash
   deploy/home/agent.sh add <id> --name <Name> --acts-for <Person>
   ```

   Add `--scopes lighting appliance` to narrow your rights. Then restart Porch. The key is never
   printed. `agent.sh connect <id>` passes it for a local client, and `agent.sh online-key <id>`
   makes a separate key for clients outside the home.
2. **Connect** as above, and call `describe_home`.

To try Porch with no hardware, `porch_gateway/start.sh` starts a home of eight simulated devices,
with demo keys in `porch_gateway/porch_config.json` (README, "Run it locally").

## A small example

The user asks: "Turn on the air purifier in the bedroom."

```text
→ describe_home()
← {"you_are": "Helper acting for Yoonchan",
   "devices": [ ..., {"device_id": "home-1/air-purifier", "name": "Air purifier", "room": "Bedroom",
                      "type": "AirPurifier", "capability": "appliance", "online": true, "platform": "lg_thinq",
                      "state": {"OnOff.OnOff": false, "AirQuality.AirQuality": "Good", ...},
                      "your_access": {"Off": "allowed", "On": "allowed"}}, ... ]}

→ request_action(device_id="home-1/air-purifier", command="On",
                 reason="Yoonchan asked for clean air in the bedroom")
← {"decision": "allowed", "reason": "Appliance action within household limits.", "executed": true,
   "verified": true, "before": {"OnOff.OnOff": false}, "predicted": {"OnOff.OnOff": true},
   "after": {"OnOff.OnOff": true}, "latency_ms": 1159, "control_path": "lg_thinq", "audit_id": "a-012"}
```

The answer to the user: "The bedroom air purifier is on. Porch confirmed it by reading the
purifier back."

Had someone else been in the bedroom while Yoonchan was out, the answer would have been
`held_for_approval` with `"waiting_for": "Wife"`. Then the agent says that the request waits for
Wife's approval, and switches nothing.
